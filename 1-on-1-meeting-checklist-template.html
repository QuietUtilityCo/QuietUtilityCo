1-on-1 Meeting Checklist for Managers

September 15, 2026

Running effective 1-on-1 meetings with your team is one of the highest-leverage activities a manager can do—yet most managers wing it. They skip them when busy, run long without direction, or leave meetings forgetting what was discussed. The result: miscommunication, missed problems, and team members who don't feel seen.

A structured 1-on-1 changes that. It signals that you value your team member's time and growth. It creates a predictable space to surface real problems instead of letting them fester. And it gives you a clear record of decisions, blockers, and progress you can reference later.

Why Your 1-on-1s Need Structure

Without a checklist, 1-on-1s drift. You spend 20 minutes on small talk, jump to urgent problems, then run out of time to discuss career development or hard feedback. The manager leaves thinking the conversation was good; the team member leaves wondering if they're performing well.

A checklist fixes this by: - Ensuring you cover what matters most — blockers, feedback, growth — not just whatever comes to mind - Creating continuity — you can reference what you discussed last week and track progress on commitments - Protecting the time — a structure keeps you on track so 30 minutes feels complete instead of rushed - Giving your team predictability — they know what to expect and can prepare with examples or problems to discuss

The 4-Part 1-on-1 Checklist

1. Check-in (5 minutes)

Start human. Ask how they're doing—not just work-wise, but life. This isn't filler; it builds trust and surfaces context you need to understand their performance. ("How's the new baby?" "Did you recover from that late-night launch?") Listen for tone; if someone's checked out or stressed, you'll know.

2. Last Week's Actions (5 minutes)

Review the commitments from your last 1-on-1: - Did they complete the project you discussed? - Did they give feedback to their peer? - Did they start learning that new skill?

Write these down in real time. If something didn't happen, understand why—was it a priority shift, blocked by someone else, or a sign of overwhelm? Don't blame; diagnose.

3. This Week's Blockers & Support (10 minutes)

Ask directly: "What's blocking you right now?" Listen for: - Technical blockers (missing access, unclear requirements) - Relationship blockers (conflict with another team, unclear communication from leadership) - Personal blockers (piling work, unclear priorities, lack of support)

For each blocker, commit to action. You solve access issues. You set expectations. You escalate conflicts. You reprioritize work if needed. Write it down and own it.

4. Growth (5-10 minutes)

This is where managers often fail. They save it for "if time" and skip it. Protect this time.

Ask: - "What skill do you want to build in the next 3 months?" - "What project would stretch you in a good way?" - "Is there feedback you want from me on how you handled that situation?"

Give specific feedback on something they did well this week. Specific beats generic ("You're great at your job" teaches nothing). Concrete teaching sticks ("The way you scoped that API work with the frontend team—you asked exactly the right questions before building—that saved us a week").

The Template: What to Track

Use this structure for every 1-on-1:

Date: [Today's date]
Team member: [Name]
Next 1-on-1: [Schedule it now]

Check-in: [Notes on how they're doing]

Commitments from last week: - [ ] Item 1 — Status: ___ - [ ] Item 2 — Status: ___

Blockers this week: 1. [Blocker] — Owner: [You or them] — Action: [Specific next step] 2. [Blocker] — Owner: [You or them] — Action: [Specific next step]

Growth focus: - Current skill goal: [What they want to improve] - Feedback from me: [What they did well] - Next stretch project: [What would grow them]

Commitments for next week: - [ ] [Action item for them] - [ ] [Action item for you]

How This Saves You Time and Improves Your Team

Managers who use a structure like this report: - Less time in status meetings — because you have the status already - Fewer surprises — blockers surface early, before they become crises - Faster promotion readiness — you notice growth earlier and can sponsor your best people - Better retention — team members feel heard and invested in

The checklist is the tool. The real work is showing up on time, listening fully, and following through on your commitments.

Start using this structure in your next 1-on-1. Write down three blockers your team member faces. Commit to removing one. Schedule their next growth conversation.

That's how you become the manager people want to work for.
