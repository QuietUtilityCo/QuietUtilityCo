9 1:1 Questions Remote Managers Should Ask

September 15, 2026

Most remote managers ask the same three questions every week: "How are you doing?" "Any blockers?" "On track for goals?" Then everyone logs off, and nothing actually changes.

The problem isn't that you're lazy. It's that weak questions produce weak signals. You get surface answers, miss early warning signs of burnout or disengagement, and never actually build the trust that keeps people on your team.

The Science of Good 1:1 Questions

Research from Harvard Business School and CultureAmp shows that managers who ask open-ended, behavior-specific questions see: - 3.2x higher retention in their direct reports - 40% fewer surprise departures after 6 months - Higher reported psychological safety scores

The mechanism is simple: specific questions make people think, which makes them feel heard.

9 Questions That Actually Work

1. "What's one thing you solved this week you're proud of?"

Why it works: Forces reflection on wins, not just completion. People remember what they built; you learn what they value.

When to ask: Monday or early Tuesday. Get them thinking about momentum before the week gets heavy.

2. "What's blocking you that I can actually help with?"

Why it works: "Any blockers?" gets "no" every time. This one assumes there ARE blockers and invites specificity.

Example response you'll hear: "The payment API integration—I've read the docs twice and the error message isn't matching the examples."

That's actionable. You can pair them with someone, find a solution, or unblock them. With "Any blockers?" you get nothing.

3. "Where do you want to grow in the next 6 months?"

Why it works: Connects their work to their future, not just the sprint. People stay for growth, not tasks.

What to listen for: If they say "I want to lead a team" and you have no path for that, you have a retention risk. Act on it.

4. "If you were running this team, what would you change?"

Why it works: Gives them psychological permission to critique without sounding like complaining. You get honest feedback. They feel invested.

Bonus: You often get genuinely good ideas.

5. "Who on the team brings out your best work?"

Why it works: Maps your team's actual chemistry. The org chart is fake; this is your real team structure.

What to do with the answer: If someone says "I work best with Maya," protect that collaboration time. If someone says "Nobody," you have a belonging problem.

6. "What's one thing you learned this week outside of work?"

Why it works: Signals you care about them as a whole person. Builds trust faster than transactional meetings.

Bonus: You learn who's burnt out (answer: nothing) vs. who's energized (answer: specific, detailed).

7. "What would make next week better than this week?"

Why it works: Concrete, future-focused. Not "Are you okay?" but "What do you actually need?"

Why people open up: It's specific and agency-restoring. They tell you real blockers, not platitudes.

8. "Tell me about a time this week when you felt frustrated."

Why it works: Frustration is the early warning system for burnout, role misalignment, or conflict. Surfacing it early prevents exit conversations.

Listening note: If they can't think of anything, ask about meetings, documentation, or tooling. Everyone has friction points.

9. "What's one decision I'm making that doesn't make sense to you?"

Why it works: Invites them to question you safely. Psychological safety is bidirectional.

Bonus: You get feedback on your own decision-making. Sometimes they're right.

How to Run These Conversations

Don't ask all nine in one meeting—that's an interrogation. Pick 2-3 per week based on what you need to know.

Structure that works: 1. Open with one big question (1, 3, or 4) 2. Listen and follow the thread—let them expand 3. Close with something actionable: "What do you need from me?"

Record the themes, not the answers. Over 4 weeks, you'll see patterns: frustration around process, excitement about a project, growth trajectory, or warning signs.

Remote-Specific: The Async Note

If your team is fully async, add a written 1:1 frame: "Send me 3 things: one win, one blocker, one thing you're learning. I'll send mine."

Written 1:1s often go deeper than video calls because people write with more thought.

The Real Win

The goal of these questions isn't to be a therapist or best friend. It's to: - Know what your people actually need - Catch problems before they cascade - Build the trust that makes feedback land - Show that you see them as humans, not slots on an org chart

Remote managers who do this see better retention, better performance, and faster decision-making. The 30-minute investment in a real 1:1 saves you months of backfill, onboarding, and knowledge loss.

Start with one new question next week. See what you learn.
