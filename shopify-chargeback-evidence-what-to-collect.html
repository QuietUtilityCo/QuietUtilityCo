What Evidence Wins Chargeback Disputes on Shopify

September 15, 2026

Chargebacks are a silent tax on Shopify sellers. You fulfill the order, the customer receives it, then they claim it never arrived—and your payment processor sides with them because your proof is disorganized.

You have 7 to 21 days to submit evidence. Most sellers lose because their proof is scattered: shipping confirmations in one tab, customer emails in Gmail, support tickets in a spreadsheet. By day 20, they're still searching.

Why Organized Evidence Wins

Payment processors like Stripe, PayPal, and Shopify Payments receive 500+ chargeback disputes daily. They spend 30–90 seconds per case. If your evidence is clear, timestamped, and labeled—proof of order, proof of fulfillment, proof of delivery—they win you the dispute without question. If it's a mess, they default to the customer.

The difference between $50 you keep and $50 + a chargeback fee you lose is organization.

The 5 Things Processors Actually Want for Physical Products

1. Proof of Shipment (Not Just an Order)

Your payment processor doesn't care that you processed the order. They care that the customer received the product.

Screenshot your shipping label with: - Carrier name (USPS, UPS, FedEx, DHL) - Tracking number - Ship date - Customer's delivery address (must match the billing address on file)

The screenshot must show the URL bar of your shipping management system (Shopify, Printful, ShipStation). A screenshot in isolation looks edited.

2. Proof of Delivery

This is where most sellers fail. Shipping ≠ delivery. The customer could claim you shipped it, but it never arrived.

Your evidence must show: - Signed delivery (signature confirmation on the tracking page) - Delivered photo (FedEx/UPS photo on delivery) - Final tracking status ("Delivered," date, time, location)

For USPS, take a screenshot of the full USPS tracking page showing "Delivered" with the date. USPS Informed Delivery also counts if it shows the item was delivered to the address.

3. Customer Confirmation of Receipt

The nuclear option: proof the customer acknowledged they received it.

This includes: - Support ticket where they asked how to use the product (means they have it) - Email reply where they asked for a refund after using it (admission it arrived) - Product review left by the customer (only possible if they received and used it) - Complaint about the product (proves delivery; proves they used it)

This single piece of evidence wins 90% of disputes outright.

4. Billing Address Match

Chargebacks happen when a customer uses a card that doesn't belong to them. If the billing address on the card in your system matches the shipping address, the card's legitimate owner authorized the purchase.

Screenshot your order management system showing: - Customer's billing address - Customer's shipping address - Payment method on file - Timestamp of the order

5. Customer Communication History

Any message thread between you and the customer is gold. It proves: - They knew the product was coming - They received it and tried to use it - They had access to it

Pull your full email thread, support ticket log, or chat history. Include the dates and timestamps.

What Winning Evidence Actually Looks Like (By Product Type)

For Dropshipped or Print-on-Demand Orders

  1. Order confirmation screenshot (order ID, customer name, address, items, date)
  2. Shipping label (carrier, tracking number, ship date, customer address)
  3. Carrier tracking screenshot showing delivery confirmation (signature/photo/final status)
  4. Any customer communication acknowledging receipt or asking about the product

For Digital Products (Software, Courses, eBooks)

Physical delivery doesn't apply. Instead, prove the customer accessed it:

  1. Account creation screenshot showing customer's email and account creation timestamp (same day as purchase or later)
  2. Customer login log showing IP address, login date, and time (within 24 hours of purchase)
  3. Platform activity screenshot showing file downloads, course lessons accessed, or software features used
  4. License key or subscription status page showing the customer's active account

This is the evidence that stops disputes cold for digital products. "Customer accessed the course on Jan 5 at 3:47 PM from IP 204.x.x.x" ends the conversation.

For Subscriptions and Recurring Charges

The customer's claim: "I never authorized this charge" or "I cancelled but they kept charging."

Your evidence: 1. Signup agreement screenshot showing customer's email and timestamp when they checked "I agree to the terms" 2. Email receipts sent to customer after each charge (your system's proof of billing) 3. Account activity showing customer logins or usage between charges 4. Cancellation request log (if they claim they cancelled—show they never asked, or show the cancellation was processed) 5. Email from customer acknowledging the charge or asking how to use the service

The Biggest Mistakes Sellers Make

Submitting proof without dates. Screenshots with no timestamp look edited. Always show the URL bar and timestamp.

Mixing unrelated evidence. Don't include customer support complaints about quality or delivery speed. Stick to proof of fulfillment.

Assuming delivery is proof of delivery. "Shipped on Jan 3" is not the same as "Delivered on Jan 5." Get proof of final delivery status.

Submitting too late. Most processors set a deadline of 7–21 days. If you submit on day 19 out of 21, you're cutting it close and you look defensive. Submit within 3–5 days.

For digital products: assuming account access is obvious. "They have a login" doesn't count. Prove they logged in after purchase with a screenshot of their last login date and IP. Prove they used a feature or downloaded the file.

How to Organize Your Evidence Right Now

  1. Create a folder for each chargeback the moment you're notified.
  2. Screenshot the full browser window (including URL bar and timestamp) for every piece of evidence.
  3. Name each file clearly: "order_confirmation_12345.png," "delivery_confirmation_12345.png," "customer_email_jan5.pdf."
  4. Write a 2-sentence summary at the top: "Customer placed order 12345 on Jan 2. UPS confirmed delivery on Jan 5."
  5. Compile into one PDF or ZIP file.
  6. Submit to your payment processor immediately.

You'll win the dispute. Organizations that take evidence seriously stop losing 80% of chargebacks they should be winning.
