5 1:1 Meeting Templates for Better Manager-Report Talks

September 15, 2026

Running effective 1:1 meetings is one of the hardest skills for new managers. You want to keep it focused, but you also need space for real conversation. You want to track action items, but you don't want to sound like a robot reading a checklist.

The difference between a manager whose team stays and one who burns out their reports often comes down to how they run 1:1s.

Why Structure Matters in 1:1s

A 1:1 without structure becomes a venting session. Or it becomes a status update. Neither builds the relationship or helps your report grow.

The best 1:1s have a predictable rhythm: the person knows what to expect, they come prepared, and both of you leave with clarity on what's next.

But structure doesn't mean rigid. It means you have a frame that you stick to, then fill it with real conversation.

Template 1: The Goal-Focused 1:1

This one works best for reports who want clear direction and measurable progress.

Opening (2 min): How are you doing this week?

Current goals (5 min): Check in on the 2-3 goals you set last time. What's moved? What's stuck? Why?

Blockers (3 min): What's in the way? Do you need anything from me?

Next goals (3 min): What are you focusing on for the next two weeks?

Closing (2 min): Anything else on your mind?

This works because it's predictable. Your report knows they need to come with a status on their goals. They'll prepare. And every meeting, you're pulling them forward.

Template 2: The Career Development 1:1

For reports who care about growth and advancement, swap out "current goals" for a deeper career conversation every 3-4 weeks.

Opening: How are you doing?

What's going well: Name one thing they did well this week.

Career direction (8 min): Where do they want to be in 12 months? What skills do they need? How can this role help them build those?

One action: Pick one concrete thing they can do in the next two weeks to move toward that goal.

Blockers and support: What do they need from you?

This template is harder because it requires you to actually listen and ask follow-up questions. But people stay longer when they see a path forward.

Template 3: The Values-Check 1:1

Some weeks, skip the goals entirely. Just check in on what matters.

Opening: How are you doing with work-life balance right now?

What energizes you: What part of your work this week felt right? What drained you?

Alignment: Do you feel like your work aligns with why you joined us?

Support: What would make this week better?

You don't need this every time. But every 4-6 weeks, stepping back to ask if someone feels good about what they're doing catches burnout early.

Template 4: The Feedback 1:1

A 1:1 focused on feedback (both ways) usually happens after a project or every 6 weeks.

Opening: I want to talk about how [project/period] went for you.

What they did well: Give specific, recent examples.

Growth area: One thing they can improve, with concrete examples.

Their feedback for you: Ask them what you could do better as a manager.

Next steps: How will they apply the feedback? When will you check back in?

Feedback without blame lands better when it's tied to specific moments. "You're not detailed enough" doesn't help. "When you shipped that feature last week, we caught three edge cases in QA that you could have caught in review" does.

Template 5: The Quick Check-In 1:1

Not every 1:1 needs to be deep. Some weeks you have 20 minutes instead of 30.

Status: What did you get done? What's next?

One thing: One thing going well + one thing that needs help.

Confirm: Do you have everything you need from me?

You can do this in 15 minutes. It's just enough to stay connected and catch problems early.

How to Run These Actually

The templates only work if you actually use them. Here's how:

  1. Use the same template each time (unless you're rotating). Predictability helps people prepare.

  2. Write it down before the meeting. Send it to them, or print it. Yes, actually print it. Seeing the structure on paper keeps you both on track.

  3. Take notes. Not a transcript—just the goals, blockers, and decisions. Send them a summary after.

  4. Do it at the same time every week. Tuesday at 2pm, not "whenever." Your report will block it, prep for it, and respect it.

  5. Keep them to 30 minutes max. The constraint forces you to prioritize what matters.

The Real Secret

The template doesn't matter as much as consistency. A simple template you actually run every week beats a fancy one you skip half the time.

Managers who have strong teams usually have two things in common: they run predictable 1:1s, and they actually listen during them. The template just makes sure you hit the important stuff and then leave room for the listening part.

Start with one template for two weeks. See which one feels natural with your team. Then stick with it.
