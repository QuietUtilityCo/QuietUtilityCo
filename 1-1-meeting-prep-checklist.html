1:1 Meeting Prep Checklist for Remote Managers

September 15, 2026

Most remote managers walk into a 1:1 with no agenda. They wing it. And it shows.

The person across the video call can feel when you haven't thought about them. They sense when you're checking email. They know when you're making it up as you go.

The fix is not complicated. It's a checklist.

Why Remote 1:1s Need More Prep Than Office 1:1s

In an office, context is ambient. You overhear conversations. You see someone stressed. You know who's stuck because they ask you questions at lunch.

Remote, there's no ambient context. Your only data is what the person tells you. So you have to create a system to hold it.

Without a system, every 1:1 starts from zero. You forget what they mentioned last month. You miss that they've been anxious about the same blocker for three weeks. You don't remember that they want to learn Python but you never asked how it's going.

A checklist changes that. It takes 5 minutes before the call and forces you to remember.

The Pre-Meeting Checklist (5 minutes)

1. Pull up last week's notes - What did they say they were working on? - What blockers did they mention? - What did you commit to doing?

2. Review your follow-ups - Did you do the thing you said you'd do? (If no, be ready to explain why.) - Did they do the thing they said they'd do? - If something slipped, why? Is it a pattern?

3. Scan Slack and calendar - Is there anything from this past week that matters? A missed deadline, a win, a frustration? - If they mentioned something in Slack, note it. It probably matters.

4. Write down three things you want to know - Not about their work. About them. What's the actual blocker? What do they need? What are they worried about?

That's it. Five minutes of prep is the difference between a meeting that matters and a status call.

The During-Meeting Checklist

Open: - "How are you doing?" (Not "how's work"—you.) - Wait for the real answer. Most people will first give a surface answer. Wait one beat. They'll say the truth.

Middle: - "What's blocking you right now?" - If they say nothing: "What would move fastest if it was gone?" - Listen. Write down what you hear. Ask one follow-up question.

Before close: - "What do you need from me?" - This is where most managers blank. Just ask it. Then be quiet. - Write down what they say. Commit to a timeline.

Close: - "Anything else?" - Then: "I'll send a summary of what we talked about."

The After-Meeting Checklist (5 minutes)

Write three things down:

  1. What matters to them (not work tasks—their actual priorities) - "Worried about AWS costs" - "Wants to own the dashboard rewrite" - "Considering MBA"

  2. One blocker (the thing preventing them from shipping) - "Waiting on design feedback" - "Unclear on success metric" - "Drowning in Slack interruptions"

  3. One need from you (what they asked for, or what you heard them need) - "Needs decision on vendor by Friday" - "Needs 2 hours uninterrupted" - "Needs sponsorship for that course"

Then create action items: - Owner: you or them - What: the specific thing - When: the date it's due

Send a summary Slack message (60 seconds): "Hey—great 1:1 today. Heard that X is your blocker. I'm going to Z by Friday. You were going to do Y. Let me know if I'm missing anything."

That message is magic. It shows you listened. It creates accountability on both sides. It gives them something to point to later.

Why This Matters for Retention

Remote employees leave because they feel unseen. They don't quit because of pay or title. They quit because they don't feel known.

A 1:1 checklist fixes that. It says: I remembered what you said last month. I thought about you before this call. I'm committing to something specific. You matter.

Teams with structured 1:1s have 40% lower voluntary turnover. Not because the manager is nicer—because the person feels managed toward their growth, not just managed at.

Implementation: Start This Week

Don't redesign your entire 1:1 process. Start small:

Week 1: Print this checklist. Before your next 1:1, spend 5 minutes on it. During the call, write down the three things. After, send a summary message.

Week 2: Do it again. This time you'll have notes from Week 1. Compare. You'll see patterns.

Week 3: You'll notice you're remembering things without looking at notes. The system is working.

By week 4, you'll wonder why you ever winged a 1:1. And your people will notice. They'll feel more seen, more unblocked, more valued.

That's when retention goes up. That's when people stay.
